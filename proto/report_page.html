Piano report

The rail of the traits plan: what each grand's parameters are, where its engine's bass attack leaves what the recorded grands do, and its tone per register against its own recordings. Built by just report (--uprights for the uprights); a warning, not a veto. Hover a graph for its values.

Out of the envelope

Per grand and band: the keys (A0–B2) where its engine's change from mf to ff lies beyond the range of the grands' recordings.

How much the bass attack brightens from mf to ff

Per key: the attack's level in a band (0.02–0.15 s, re 200–500 Hz) at ff minus at mf. The grey band spans the grands' recordings (bands within 10 dB of a recording's hiss left out). Each set's own mf and ff layers, so the speeds differ between sets.

The attack against its own recording

Per key: the engine's attack minus the piano's recording in the same band (dB, both re 200–500 Hz, at the Recording position). Zero is a match; above is brighter than the recording. Piano and band as chosen above.

The tone per register

Within a note, its octave bands over the first second re the band of its fundamental, the engine minus its recording (dB, the median over the recorded keys; both channels' power, at the layers' velocities): a register's brightness, apart from its loudness. Red: brighter than the recording, blue: darker. Below, the lowest octaves' fundamentals re partials 2–4 (too strong where a board fit holds its response flat below its data). tone_check.py; steps 143 and 147.

Level along the keyboard

Each key's level at its ff (the peak over the first 0.2 s at the Recording position, knock off), in dB re the piano's median: the level it plays at every velocity, since the engine plays a velocity at the speed where the key sounds a set level under its own ff. The keys' evenness, not the pianos' loudness against each other. The pianos shown follow the traits' legend below.

The board's frequency response

Fitted from each grand's recordings (smooth over frequency), normalized to its maximum; dotted where the recordings had no data (held flat). The board's color. The pianos shown follow the traits' legend below.

The board's correction per key

Where one shared response does not fit: per anchor key (rows, bass at the bottom) and frequency (columns, log scale), dB the fit adds to the response there. Red: more, blue: less. Large or patterned corrections are where the fit may have absorbed something.
